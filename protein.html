You can actually eat too much protein

Feb 2023

Based on modern culture, one could think that we need to constantly eat more protein. The media is obsessed with protein powders, vegan vs. animal protein, high-protein snacks, and so on and so forth. But protein, when consumed without any fat or carbohydrates, can be unhealthy.

The main function of protein is to rebuild tissues as they break down, playing a vital role in the maintenance of healthy metabolism, movement, DNA replication, response to stimuli and many other processes. Without protein, we would not survive. But, protein is not a main energy source: instead, humans get over 75% of their energy from carbohydrates and fat. Yes, protein can be used as energy when starving, but that is like lighting one’s furniture on fire to heat up one’s home.

Carbs are digested quite simply, by being broken down into monosaccharides and absorbed into the bloodstream, making them a source of immediate energy. Fats require a bit more work, as they are digested into individual fatty acids, built back up into fats in the form of triglycerides (three fatty acids chained to a glycerol, the standard shape of fat in our bodies) and stored for future use. They are, therefore, primarily a source of long-term energy.

When we don’t eat any fat or carbs, and deplete our stores, our body eventually runs out of fuel sources. Eating primarily only protein isn’t enough: rabbit starvation, a term coined by hunters trying to survive in winter woods only on rabbits (very lean animals, basically pure protein), is when one continues to lose weight even if consuming enough calories.

That is why every historic civilization has a grain as the centerpiece of their diet, or the food that ordinary people survived on. In Africa, it is yams and millet. In China, rice and noodles are the fan, which are balanced with cai, or everything else. In Ancient Greece, *opsa (*the staple starch) was wheat, served with sitos (something to season it with).

Even traditionally high-protein diets, such as keto, paleo and carnivore diets, all include carbs or fat in some capacity. When you overeat protein in proportion to everything else, you can get protein poisoning.

Too much protein affects renal function, stressing your kidneys and eventually impacting your body’s waste removal process, developing into protein toxicity. In normal conditions, once you reach a level of protein needed to repair all tissues for a day, any protein surplus is unnecessary and is excreted in urea. If this process is under stress, your body’s ability to do anything else gets impaired, including functioning normally and successfully fighting off disease. This causes fatigue, weakness, nausea, aches and fluctuating blood pressure. As it were, more is not always better.
